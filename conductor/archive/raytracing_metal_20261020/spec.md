# Spec: Ray-traced sun shadows and ambient occlusion with Metal

## Overview
Halo CE lights its levels with baked lightmaps, and its objects with a few dynamic lights and a blob or stencil shadow. This track adds Metal ray tracing to the macOS app's renderer. Rays are traced against the level and the objects to give sharp, correct sun shadows and ray-traced ambient occlusion, layered over the game's own lighting.

The M1 Max has no ray-tracing hardware: Metal's intersection runs on its shader cores. So the track starts with a **spike** that measures the cost on the M1 Max before anything else is built, and it goes on only if the spike meets the frame budget. Everything is drawn in the host's Metal backend; the game and the netcode are unchanged.

## Functional Requirements

### Phase 1: the spike (go / no-go)
1. Build a Metal primitive acceleration structure from the structure BSP's render geometry, sent once when a BSP loads.
2. At half resolution, trace one shadow ray per pixel toward the sun, from depth reconstructed from the depth buffer, and darken the frame by the result.
3. Measure on bloodgulch and a campaign level at 3840×2160 on the M1 Max: the acceleration structure's build time, the trace pass's GPU time, and the effect on p50 and p99.
4. Record the numbers and a go / no-go decision in `docs/macos_host.md`. **Go** means the trace pass fits in about 3 ms of GPU time, so p99 stays ≤ 8.33 ms. On a no-go, the track stops after phase 1, and screen-space AO (requirement 12) is taken on alone.

### The scene
5. The guest sends what the host needs to trace through the command ring: the BSP's geometry when it loads or switches, the camera's matrices, the sun's direction, and each drawn object's model and transform each frame.
6. Objects are instances in an instance acceleration structure: one primitive structure per model (built once and cached), placed by transform, and refitted each frame. Skinned models (bipeds) use a refitted structure from their posed vertices, or a capsule proxy per bone if that is cheaper (the spike decides).
7. Transparent and alpha-tested surfaces (foliage, fences, glass) are handled with an intersection function that reads the texture's alpha.

### The effects
8. **Sun shadows:** a ray-traced shadow mask at half resolution, upscaled with a depth-aware filter, applied to the sun's share of the lighting. The lightmap's existing shadow is kept, so the mask darkens only dynamic objects' shadows and whatever the lightmap missed.
9. **Ambient occlusion:** short-range rays (a few per pixel, at half resolution), accumulated over frames, with the same filter.
10. **Denoising:** a spatial and temporal filter of our own (with motion vectors from the camera and objects' transforms). MetalFX's denoiser is used instead if macOS 26 provides one that suits, decided in the spike.
11. The effects are combined before the game's transparent geometry and the HUD, so neither is shadowed.
12. **Screen-space AO** as a cheaper alternative (no ray tracing), selectable separately.

### Settings
13. `video.ray_traced_shadows` and `video.ray_traced_ao`, off by default; `video.ambient_occlusion` with values off, screen-space and ray-traced, off by default. Each has an environment variable and a comment for `config.toml`, is listed in the settings table and is on the port's settings screen (generated by `tools/port_settings.py`).
14. `debug.raytrace_view` shows the shadow mask, the AO or the acceleration structure's heat map, for tuning.

## Non-Functional Requirements
- **Performance:** with every effect on, bloodgulch at 3840×2160 keeps p99 ≤ 8.33 ms (120 fps) on the M1 Max. If it cannot, the settings screen offers the half-resolution and quarter-resolution modes that do.
- **Display only:** nothing the guest simulates changes; `HALO_PORT_NETWORK_VERSION` is not raised. With the settings off, frames are identical to today's golden frames.
- **Repeatable:** with `debug.frame_clock`, the temporal filter's output is the same every run, so golden frames can test it.
- **Safety:** geometry and per-frame data from the guest are validated by the ring's decoder (sizes, indices, offsets, finite numbers).
- **Source changes:** `source/` changes are minimal, each marked `port:`.
- **Coverage:** >80% for the new host code and the guest encoder.

## Acceptance Criteria
1. The spike's measurements and its go / no-go are recorded in `docs/macos_host.md`.
2. Unit tests: the ring's new commands decode and reject bad input; an acceleration structure built from a known mesh gives the expected hits (XCTest); the denoiser converges on a static synthetic scene.
3. Golden frames, with the frame clock: a Warthog's shadow on bloodgulch at noon; AO in a corridor on a campaign level; foliage's alpha-tested shadow; with the settings off, the existing goldens unchanged.
4. The benchmark with every effect on meets the performance target, recorded in `docs/macos_host.md`.
5. The settings are documented; `docs/macos_host.md` describes the ray tracing passes.

## Out of Scope
- Ray-traced reflections, global illumination and path tracing (possible later tracks, after the spike's numbers).
- Replacing the baked lightmaps.
- Upscaling (MetalFX upscaling) as a feature of its own.
- Any non-macOS build, and any change to gameplay or the netcode.
